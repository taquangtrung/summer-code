# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good faith, and write as though they are in the room.

## What is expected

- **Engage with the argument, not the person.** "This breaks resume on a compacted session" is useful. "Did you even read the architecture doc" is not, and it is not made useful by being correct about the doc.
- **Take disagreement about technical decisions as normal.** This project treats its documents as contracts and expects them to be challenged when the code disagrees with them. A patch that argues a design contract is wrong is a welcome patch.
- **Accept that maintainer time is finite.** A review may be slow, and a change may be declined because nobody wants to maintain it rather than because it is bad. Both are ordinary outcomes.
- **Respect a decision once it is made**, including one that went against you. Reopening a settled question needs new information, not more emphasis.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility toward anyone, in issues, pull requests, commit messages, or anywhere else this project is discussed.
- Demeaning, insulting, or derogatory remarks, and personal or political attacks.
- Comments targeting someone's identity: age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity or orientation.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, or persistently reopening a settled decision to exhaust the people on the other side of it.

## Scope

This applies in every space the project uses: the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages and code comments, and any venue where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Raise it privately with the maintainer at <taquangtrungvn@gmail.com>. Reports are read by the maintainer alone and kept confidential, including from the person reported.

Describe what happened and link to it if it happened in public. You will get an acknowledgement, and you will be told what was decided. You are not expected to have a view on what the consequence should be.

The maintainer is currently one person, so there is no independent body to appeal to. If the report concerns the maintainer, that is worth saying plainly in the report, and a public issue is a legitimate route when a private one would go to the person being reported.

## Consequences

The maintainer may edit or delete offending content, close or lock a thread, decline a contribution, or block an account. The response fits what happened: most of what this document covers is handled once, quietly, with a note about why.
